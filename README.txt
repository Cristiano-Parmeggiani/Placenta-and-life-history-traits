Title of the article: Placental Interdigitation Is Associated with Reproductive Pace across Mammals

Abstract:
The placenta is a transient organ mainly derived from fetal tissue that is essential for establishing metabolic and immune interactions that support healthy pregnancies in eutherian mammals. Across mammals, placentas show high morphological diversity in both the degree of tissue invasiveness and in the complexity of tissue integration between maternal and fetal tissues. This variation is thought to reflect strong selective pressures on reproductive strategies, balancing maternal investment, offspring development, resource transfer, and reproductive pace.  Previous studies that explored the relationship between placental morphology and mammalian life history often used small datasets and focused mainly on placental invasiveness. Placental interdigitation has received less attention. Here, we analyze 509 mammalian species to examine how three life-history traits (gestation length, interbirth interval, and relative litter mass) are associated with placental morphology. We also test whether placental morphology covaries with broader multivariate life-history structure using phylogenetic principal component analyses (pPCA). We found that placental interdigitation was more strongly associated with reproductive timing than placental invasiveness. Villous placentas were associated with shorter gestation lengths than labyrinthine placentas, and were also associated with the longest interbirth intervals. Trabecular placentas showed the shortest intervals. Placental invasiveness showed little association with life-history variation, except for a weak difference in interbirth interval between endotheliochorial and hemochorial species. Neither dimension was associated with relative litter mass. Our results suggest that placental interdigitation is closely associated with reproductive timing (gestation length and interbirth interval), whereas neither interdigitation nor invasiveness is associated with reproductive investment at birth (relative litter mass).
Keywords: placental evolution, placenta interdigitation, placenta invasiveness, life-history traits, maternal-fetal conflict, comparative phylogenetics, reproductive strategies, eutherian mammals
